# Cue

Cue is a hackathon MVP (Immerse the Bay, 2026-11-14). A person signals interest
with a backward swipe on the temple touch strip of Mentra Live smart glasses.
If another person in the same place signals within 15 seconds, the web app
creates a match with the closest one. The match card shows the other person's approved profile, the
encounter time, and a coarse venue label. Matched people can text each other
inside the app. An optional "Find common ground" card, generated by Claude, shows
things supported by both approved profiles and one optional opening question that
a person can drop into the message box with "Use this opener".

This is a hackathon prototype, not a production service.

## How it works

1. Each person creates a profile and chooses, field by field, what a match can
   see. A profile can include age, gender, city, what they are looking for,
   work or school, education, MBTI, personality cards and, optionally, ethnicity
   and drinking, smoking and drugs (those four, and work or school and education,
   are off by default). The server derives one of twelve characters (for example
   "The Night Owl") from the cards. External social links are not collected.
2. Both turn on Encounter mode. Cue needs to know they are in the same place:
   either both check into the same venue (anyone can "Host a place" and show its
   QR code; others scan it or type its six-character code), or both phones
   report a location, and the two are within 40 m allowing for each phone's
   reported accuracy. The open Cue page reports location only while Encounter
   mode is on.
3. Person A makes a backward swipe on Mentra Live glasses. The swipe is
   submitted as interest. Person B taps "Interested" in the web app, or
   (experimental, not verified on a device) double taps Meta Adventurer glasses
   paired to an iPhone: the signed-in web page receives the headset media
   command and sends it as interest (`headset_remote`).
4. If a signal arrives while another person in the same place has a pending
   signal under 15 seconds old (server receipt time), the server creates one
   match with the closest such person and consumes both signals. Otherwise the
   signal expires after 15 seconds and nothing is shared. The match records only
   a venue name or "Nearby", never a location.
5. The two people can text each other in the app while the match is active.
   Unmatch or Block ends the match and deletes the conversation.
6. "Find common ground" is optional and only shown to the person who asks.
   Claude sees only the fields each person shared with AI assist on, never age,
   gender, ethnicity, drinking, smoking or drugs, and never chat messages. It
   writes one opener and sends nothing.
7. On a match Cue also says one or two short sentences about it in a casual
   friend's voice ("Your wingman"), through the glasses when they are the phone's
   audio device, otherwise the phone speaker. It is requested automatically,
   needs AI assist on for both people, and is built from the same checked shared
   items plus the other person's display name and a venue's name. With nothing in
   common to go on (a name-only guest, say) it is a fixed line. The "Wingman
   voice" switch on Encounter turns the speech off in that browser.

The one-sided interest stays invisible to the other person. This is not a
two-person private session any more: Cue pairs a signal with the closest other
person who signalled in the same place, so in a crowd it can pick someone you did
not mean. The match screen offers "Not who you meant? Unmatch". See
`docs/place-matching.md` for the rule, what is stored and the limits.

See `docs/spec.md` for the original product behavior (kept as written; place
matching reverses its "never choose a random or nearest account" and "exactly two
people" rules on the owner's instruction), `docs/profiles-and-chat.md` for
profiles, the character and chat (also added later, on the owner's instruction),
and `docs/architecture.md` for the technical contract.

### Fastest way in: a guest account (demo only)

On the sign-in or sign-up page, "New here? Just say hi": tap "Say hi" and say
"Hi, my name is Sam", or type "Sam" under "Or type it" and tap "Continue as
guest". You get an account named Sam with no email or password. On Mentra
glasses the mini app listens while unlinked: say "Hi, my name is Sam" and the
glasses link themselves to a new guest account, and "Open Cue as Sam" signs the
phone in. A guest who signs out cannot get back in. Guest accounts are for the
demo and are deleted afterwards. Switch it off with `CUE_GUESTS_ENABLED=off`;
details, limits and the removal checklist are in `docs/guest-accounts.md`.
Spoken entry sends audio to the browser vendor's speech service (Cue receives
only the text); it has not been tried on real phones or glasses.

A name-only guest gives the wingman nothing specific to say, so they hear the
fixed line. To get a specific tip, both people add a shared interest on Profile.

### Try it with two phones

1. Open the site (or `http://localhost:3000`) on two phones, each signed in as a
   different demo account (see below) or as a guest, in its own browser profile.
2. Turn on "Encounter mode" on both. Allow location when asked.
3. Make both phones "Ready" in the "Where you are" card. Indoors, prefer a venue
   check-in: on one phone, "Host a place", name it, tap "Host this place"; on
   the other, scan its QR code or type its code under "Enter a venue code" and tap
   "Check in". Phone location alone may be too rough indoors (it must be 100 m
   accurate or better, and the two within 40 m after allowing for accuracy).
4. Tap "Interested" on both (or swipe on the glasses on one) within 15 seconds
   of each other. Both screens show "It's mutual."
5. "Demo controls" has "Reset demo" and "End demo" between runs.

On the hosted site the demo is best done with a venue check-in when indoors.
`docs/demo.md` has the full script.

### Hardware status

**Mentra Live.** The real-swipe checkpoint was **met** (2026-11-14), under the old
two-person session model: seven real swipes reached the live server under the
wearer's account in about 0.2 to 0.3 s and two matches completed. On 2026-11-15
the owner reported that a real Mentra swipe reached the live site under
place-based matching too; the two accounts were already matched, so no new match
was created in that run. A new match by place from real hardware has **not been
verified**. On the 2026-11-14 unit the swipe's gesture name was not the
built-in default, so the mini app's calibration override was needed. Hair
adjustments, the pocket test, the media-control conflict and glasses audio are
not yet recorded. See `docs/hardware-check.md`.

The Mentra mini app can be installed from the site itself, without a laptop: open
`/mentra` on a second screen and scan its QR code with the Mentra app's developer
scanner. The owner reported (2026-11-15) that this worked on their phone with no
laptop; the exact steps and versions were not recorded. `docs/hardware-check.md`
step 2 has the laptop fallback. The new voice features (spoken guest sign-up and
the spoken match line) have not been tried on any glasses.

**Meta Adventurer.** Only partly verified. The web page listens for headset media
commands (double tap by default). The owner reported (2026-11-15) that a real
Meta double tap reached the live site under place-based matching; the accounts
were already matched, so no new match was created, and the steps in
`docs/meta-glasses-check.md` (the command list, the other gestures, background
and locked behavior, audio) were not recorded. Pairing a Meta tap with a
Mentra swipe into a new match by location has not been verified. The signal
shows that some Bluetooth headset sent a media command, not that Meta glasses
did, and the gesture is a tap pattern, not a swipe. See
`docs/meta-glasses-check.md`.

## Run it locally

Prerequisites: Node 22 or newer, pnpm, and Docker (for the local Supabase stack).

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm stack:start    # local Supabase in Docker; writes apps/web/.env.local
pnpm dev            # http://localhost:3000
```

Other commands:

```bash
pnpm stack:reset    # re-apply migrations and seed data
pnpm stack:stop     # stop the local Supabase stack
pnpm --filter @cue/web build
```

Seeded synthetic demo accounts (password for all three: `cue-demo-123`):

- `alex@cue.demo`
- `sam@cue.demo` (Alex and Sam share some interests)
- `riley@cue.demo` (a third account, for checking what happens when someone else signals)

All three have full synthetic profiles. Alex fills the sensitive fields and
shares none of them.

Set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` in `apps/web/.env.local` to enable the Claude card.
Without it, matching still works and the card shows a retry or error state.

## Environment variables

Values for the three Supabase variables are written by `pnpm stack:start`.
Put the others in `apps/web/.env.local` by hand. Never commit real values.

| Variable | Required | Where it is used | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` | Yes | Browser and server | Local stack URL. Written by `pnpm stack:start`. |
| `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` | Yes | Browser and server | Anon key. Written by `pnpm stack:start`. |
| `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` | Yes | Server only | Never sent to a browser or the glasses. Written by `pnpm stack:start`. |
| `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` | For the Claude card | Server only | Without it the card shows a retry or error state; matching still works. |
| `ANTHROPIC_MODEL` | No | Server only | Default `claude-opus-5-5`. |
| `WINGMAN_TIMEOUT_MS` | No | Server only | Default `15000`. |
| `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` | No | Server only | Used by tests to point at a local fake Anthropic server. |
| `ANTHROPIC_FALLBACKS` | No | Server only | Set to `off` to disable the server-side refusal fallback parameter. |
| `HYPE_TIMEOUT_MS` | No | Server only | Time allowed for the spoken match line's model call. Default `8000`; after that the fixed line is used. |
| `CUE_GUESTS_ENABLED` | No | Server only | Demo guest accounts are on unless this is `off`, `false` or `0`. Set it to `off` on Vercel and redeploy to stop new guests; see `docs/guest-accounts.md`. |

## Tests

```bash
pnpm typecheck && pnpm lint && pnpm test   # all workspace packages
pnpm --filter @cue/web build
pnpm test:e2e                              # Playwright; needs the local stack
```

The end-to-end tests use a fake Anthropic server and never call the real API.

Live Claude check (needs a real `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; skipped in the normal test
run). It sends the synthetic seeded-style pair, a no-overlap pair and a profile
containing instructions to the real model and prints raw and validated output:

```bash
WINGMAN_LIVE=1 ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-ant-... \
  pnpm --filter @cue/web exec vitest run src/server/ai/live.test.ts
```

The live model call has not been verified yet because no API key has been
available for it.

## Limitations

- Normally one pair of glasses: one person uses the glasses, the other the web
  app. The Meta route allows two glasses wearers but is not verified on a device.
- `headset_remote` is not proof of device: any Bluetooth headset sending a media
  command on that phone would trigger it. The Meta gesture is a tap pattern, not
  the backward swipe.
- Matching pairs a signal with the closest other person who signalled within 15
  seconds in the same place. In a crowd this can be the wrong person; being near
  someone does not prove who they meant. A wrong match shows the other person
  what you share with matches until you unmatch.
- Phone location indoors is often off by 10 to 30 m, and a modified client can
  fake it. It is kept only while Encounter mode is on (deleted on mode off or
  after 10 minutes without an update) and never shown, saved with a match or sent
  to Claude.
- The Cue page must be open for location. It is expected not to update in the
  background or with an iPhone locked; that is not verified on a real phone.
- Venues are self-reported: anyone can create one, and a code can be shared
  outside the place. The QR code has no print layout, and signing up (rather than
  signing in) from a QR link loses the venue code.
- One lock serialises all matching decisions: fine for a demo, a throughput limit.
- Place matching has not been tried by two real phones matching, and no new
  match by place has been completed by real glasses (a real swipe and a real
  Meta double tap reached the live site on 2026-11-15, with the accounts already
  matched).
- No identity verification.
- Guest accounts (demo only) are open sign-up while the switch is on, with an
  in-memory per-address limit and a daily cap of 300 that is not atomic. A guest
  who signs out cannot get back in. The spoken route sends audio to the browser
  vendor's speech service, and the Mentra mini app holds the glasses' microphone
  while it is not linked. None of it is tried on a real phone or glasses. See
  `docs/guest-accounts.md`.
- The spoken match line is requested automatically on a match and sends Claude
  the other person's display name (the one place a name is sent). Its real
  wording with a live key, and speech through phones or glasses, are unverified.
- Chat is text only, inside the app. There is no feed and there are no push
  notifications. Live updates are in-app only.
- Chat cannot stop someone typing their own contact details. Removing social
  links and not linking URLs lowers exposure; it does not enforce anything.
- There is no message reporting or moderation beyond Unmatch and Block.
- Ethnicity and substance use are sensitive. They are optional, hidden by default
  and never sent to Claude, but they are stored in the database once filled in,
  and this project has no privacy policy. Use synthetic data for demos.
- Age is self-declared and not verified. Nothing about a person is verified.
- The chat send limit is in memory and works only on a single server instance.
- The rate limit for the Claude card is in memory and works only on a single
  server instance.
- The live Claude call is not verified until an API key is supplied, and the
  fuller input (cards, character, MBTI, location and so on) has not been run
  live, nor has the spoken match line. Automated tests use a fake model.
- The profile, character and chat screens have not been tried on the real phones.
- The Mentra swipe was verified on one real unit under the old session model, and
  a real swipe and a real Meta double tap were reported reaching the live site
  under place matching (see Hardware status); no new match by place has been
  completed by real hardware, and the Meta route's details were not recorded.
- The end-to-end tests cover the server path (device-token and `headset_remote`
  events). They do not test a physical swipe or tap.
- Hackathon MVP. Not production software.
